# Institutional Deference Evolution

Every analytical claim in a Kallipolis partnership artifact is authored by a named institutional source — not generated by an LLM, not produced by a heuristic, not composed from skills overlap. This is the architectural commitment that makes the artifact defensible to a community college coordinator's procurement officer, to a Chancellor's Office reviewer, and to a regional Center of Excellence. The product does not present opinions; it presents the structure that institutional authorities have already published, joined into a coherent whole.

The commitment was not designed in one stroke. It was realized progressively across thirteen commits — the C-series — that incrementally replaced LLM-generated and skills-overlap-derived signals with institutional crosswalks. This document is the durable record of that evolution.

## The source-attribution chain

The chain that authors layer 1 of every partnership claim, in order:

1. **TOP-CIP crosswalk** — Chancellor's Office mapping from the per-college 6-digit TOP code (the institution's own classification of every course) to the federal CIP (Classification of Instructional Programs) taxonomy.
2. **CIP-SOC crosswalk** — BLS / NCES mapping from CIP programs to SOC (Standard Occupational Classification) codes. The federal authority for "what occupations does this program prepare students for."
3. **NAICS-OES industry-occupation matrix** — BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics National Industry-Occupation Matrix, keyed by NAICS-4 (and falling through to NAICS-3 / NAICS-2 at BLS's published resolution). The federal authority for "what occupations does this industry actually employ."
4. **COE regional demand projections** — California Centers of Excellence published wage, employment, and growth-rate figures by SOC × COE region. The state authority for "what does this region's labor market actually need."

Every analytical claim in a partnership artifact draws from one or more of these. The atlas surface attribution sections name each source by publication, and the audit (`tools/docs-audit/audit.py`) verifies that every node-type schema and every analytical edge in the graph traces to one of them.

## The C-series

| Commit | Subject |
|---|---|
| C1 | Carry institutional source attribution end-to-end |
| C2 | Replace LLM occupation selector with deterministic crosswalk-depth ranker |
| C3 | Remove LLM department-relevance filter |
| C4 | Rewrite query.py NL search prompt to PREPARES_FOR-rooted ranking |
| C5 | Rewrite PARTNERSHIP_ALIGNMENT precompute to PREPARES_FOR-rooted ranking |
| C6 | Add INSTITUTIONAL CHAIN block to narrative context |
| C7 | Rewrite NARRATIVE_PROMPT to walk the empirical chain |
| C8 | Add 4 institutional-deference eval rules |
| C9 | Canonical Abbott Vascular CI fixture, drop turnkey overclaim pattern |
| C10 | Atlas: surface institutional source attribution in ProposalCard |
| C11 | Make demoted role of skills visible in gather.py docstrings |
| C12 | Filter partnership surfaces to crosswalk-supported (`aligned_course_count >= 1`) |
| C13 | Anchor layer 1 (HIRES_FOR) on BLS OEWS NAICS-SOC matrix |

C1 established the principle. C2–C5 replaced LLM and skills-overlap selectors in the analytical computations. C6–C9 made the chain legible in the narrative voice and the eval gates. C10–C12 closed the surface — the atlas now names each source visibly, and the picker filter ensures coordinators only see partnership opportunities where the institutional pathway exists. C13 closed the last hole: layer 1 (`Employer-[:HIRES_FOR]->Occupation`) had been LLM-curated against a region-wide CTE-pathway pool that was institutionally sourced but not industry-anchored. C13 derives `HIRES_FOR` deterministically from the BLS OEWS National Industry-Occupation Matrix at the deepest NAICS resolution BLS publishes for the employer's industry: every SOC published at `pct_total > 0` for the employer's `naics4` becomes a `HIRES_FOR` edge, with the published share persisted as the `pct_total` edge property. No LLM in the loop on the inclusion decision. The LLM's role is reduced to a parallel `IDENTITY_HIRES_FOR` overlay that picks 3–8 SOCs from within the same NAICS-bounded pool to mark which roles a specific employer most characteristically hires for; that overlay is a refinement signal, never the inclusion gate. The result: an employer with a `naics4` and no successful website enrichment still gets the BLS-derived `HIRES_FOR` set and surfaces in partnership reports, but loses the per-employer identity specificity until enrichment succeeds.

After C13, the source-attribution chain is consistent end-to-end across every analytical claim in the artifact.

## What the C-series is not

It is not a feature roadmap. The C-series doesn't add new functionality — it tightens existing functionality so that what was already expressed institutionally becomes expressed *only* institutionally. Every commit removes a hidden LLM judgment or a skills-overlap shortcut and replaces it with a citation to an external source. The product surface is largely unchanged; what changed is the defensibility of every claim it presents.

It is also not a refactoring archive. Future architectural changes that incrementally tighten institutional grounding may extend the series (C14, C15…) — but only if they hold to the same shape: replacing a non-institutional signal with an institutional one, with a named publication or crosswalk as the new authority. Other refactors live elsewhere.
